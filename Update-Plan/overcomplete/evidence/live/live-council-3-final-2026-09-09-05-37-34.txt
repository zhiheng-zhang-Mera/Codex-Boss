Final Synthesis: Research Artifact Store Naming

After reviewing all proposals and peer reviews, I find that Proposal B's "ReproStore" emerges as the most coherent and technically grounded recommendation. Below is the evidence-based reasoning.

Observed Evidence vs. Inference

Proposal A explicitly states in its peer review that "ResearchVault" is "平庸且辨识度低" (mediocre and low-distinction), yet paradoxically promotes it as the cross-disciplinary fallback in its final recommendation. This internal contradiction is reproducible from the text and undermines Proposal A's consistency.

Proposal B provides the clearest architectural reasoning: it distinguishes "Hub" (gateway/portal) from "Store" (passive object storage), correctly identifying that "ReproHub" implies a dynamic interface unsuited for a backend artifact repository. Its synthesized "ReproStore" directly addresses the reproducibility pain point while fixing its own candidate's overpromise.

Proposal C offers "ResearchRepo" as the safest choice but its own peer review acknowledges genericity as a flaw. No evidence supports that "Repo" distinguishes artifact storage from code repositories (VCS) or data stores—a logical boundary error.

Resolving Conflicts
Conflict	Resolution
Provenance lineage vs. Reproducibility storage vs. Generic repository	"ReproStore" is the most precise: it names the primary use case (reproducibility) and the storage abstraction (store), avoiding the overreach of "Provenance" (which implies full DAG/lineage tracking beyond basic artifact storage) and the under-specification of "ResearchRepo."
"Hub" vs. "Store" interface definition	Proposal B's objection is well-founded. "Hub" connotes active mediation, routing, or portal functionality. A research artifact store that serves as a passive, durable object repository should use "Store" or "Vault"—not "Hub."
Preserved Minority Views

The following credible minority positions deserve mention despite not being selected:

MethodCache (Proposal B, peer review) – Identifies a legitimate sub-use-case: low-latency, read-only temporary storage for intermediate pipeline artifacts. This is not a replacement for the primary artifact store but a valid complementary tier in a storage hierarchy.

Athenaeum/Chronicle (Proposal A, peer review) – The classical register may suit long-term archival preservation contexts, particularly for humanities or historical research data where engineering connotations are less relevant.

LabLedger (Proposal C, peer review) – Introduces accountability/audit-trail framing that could be valuable in compliance-heavy or collaborative research settings. The accounting-confusion risk is overstated; research artifacts are indeed tracked transactions (versions, checkpoints, provenance records). A paired qualifier (e.g., "LabLedger Store") could recover this framing.

Final Recommendation

Adopt "ReproStore" as the primary name for the research artifact store. It:

Directly targets the core value proposition: reproducibility verification.

Maintains a clear, honest abstraction: a store, not a portal or gateway.

Avoids over-promising lineage-tracking capabilities (unlike "Provenance").

Escapes the generic, collision-prone nature of "Repo."

Works across computational and non-computational disciplines without requiring specialized vocabulary.
